About Pl4nck

Pl4nck helps quants in banks and asset managers prepare their models for what comes next: an honest view of where quantum computing stands, a no-regret roadmap, and validated model kits they can use today. It was built by a practising quant with a background in model validation, structured products and regulatory frameworks.

The no-regret principle

For most finance workloads, a real quantum advantage needs error-corrected machines that are not expected before the 2030s. Waiting is still not neutral. The prerequisites are the same either way: clean data, classical benchmarks, variance reduction, reproducible runs and validated documentation. Any quantum algorithm will be measured against them, and they pay off on their own.

So every roadmap Pl4nck builds starts with steps worth doing even if quantum computing never delivers. Quantum comes last, with a dated go/no-go based on resource estimates rather than announcements. The model library delivers those first steps.

  1. TodayYour current model, priced and documented
  2. BenchmarksClosed forms and reference values, errors reported
  3. EfficiencyVariance reduction, convergence study, AAD Greeks
  4. Model riskRicher models compared, findings closed
  5. Quantum readinessReduced case on a simulator, resource estimate, go/no-go

Example for Monte Carlo pricing. Roadmaps differ by use case.

How the validated kits are built

Independent references

Reference values are computed once with QuantLib, cross-checked against independent closed forms, and stored as data in the kit. The prototype never imports QuantLib, and a test checks it: comparing them is a real check. You do not need QuantLib to run anything.

Pre-registered criteria

Every test has its acceptance criterion fixed before the code is written: error bounds, standard errors, convergence rates, no-arbitrage shapes, limits. Criteria are never loosened to make a test pass.

Tested as you would run it

Each kit is installed and tested in a clean environment before release, and every test shipped to users runs again automatically at each change of the library, on two Python versions.

Documentation you can file

Model documentation aligned with SR 11-7 and the ECB guide, pre-filled with theory, assumptions, limits and test results, in Word (native equations) and LaTeX. Shaded sections are left for your institution.

The conventions nobody tests. Day counts, holiday calendars and schedules ship with every kit, checked against QuantLib on thousands of cases, including every holiday from 2000 to 2050. Building them surfaced an error in QuantLib's own French calendar, which fixes Ascension and Whit Monday on set dates although both move with Easter.

What it does not do

  • It does not touch your systems. Pl4nck does not run your models or connect to anything inside your firm. Kits run on your machine.
  • It does not take your code or data. You describe your model family in generic terms; nothing is uploaded.
  • It does not replace your validation. Kits are tested before release, but your team reviews and adapts them to its data, standards and policy, and owns any regulatory sign-off.
  • It does not promise quantum advantage. If quantum computing is not the right answer for your use case, the verdict says so.
  • It does not sell hardware. Pl4nck is vendor-neutral, with no sponsored placements.
  • It does not cover post-quantum cryptography beyond a pointer. That migration is urgent, but it is a security programme, not a modelling one.

Who it is for

Model validators, quant R&D, front-office quants and structurers, risk managers, innovation teams, and managers planning skills and budget. No quantum background is needed.

How it works

The verdict, roadmap and custom variants are generated by an AI model (Claude, by Anthropic) guided by a curated reference base: the technology mapping, reference roadmaps and the model cards. AI answers can be wrong: treat them as a well-informed first draft. Validated kits are different: they are written and tested once, and downloading one involves no AI.

Pl4nck is a free research prototype. With your consent, your answers and the conversation are stored for 12 months to improve it; downloading a validated kit stores nothing about you. Do not share confidential information. See the privacy notice.
