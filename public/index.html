The road to useful quantum finance

Quantum amplitude estimation makes the Monte Carlo error fall as 1/N instead of 1/√N. The theory is sound; what is missing is error-corrected qubits, by about two orders of magnitude for derivatives pricing.

  1. Today to 2028Demonstrated so far Dozens logical qubitsHundreds to a few thousand physical Pilots on small instances: portfolio and collateral optimisation, toy option pricing on simulators. Quantum-inspired methods already run on classical hardware. No proven advantage.
  2. 2029 to 2030Vendor roadmaps 100 to 200 logical qubitsThousands to tens of thousands physical First error-corrected runs of finance algorithms on reduced problems: amplitude estimation on simple payoffs, optimisation at larger sizes. Research value; an advantage is unlikely.
  3. 2031 to 2034Vendor roadmaps 1,000 to 2,000 logical qubitsOrder of magnitude Amplitude estimation at meaningful sizes for VaR and credit portfolio studies; the first serious comparisons with GPU Monte Carlo. Advantage case by case, still to be shown.
  4. 2035 and beyondResource estimates About 7,500 logical qubitsPhysical count depends on error rates Advantage on derivatives pricing: autocalls and TARFs, each priced in about a second.

The threat arrives before the benefit. Breaking RSA-2048 is estimated to need fewer than a million noisy qubits, far fewer than pricing at scale. Post-quantum migration of your systems comes first, whatever your modelling plans.

Sources: IBM roadmap (Starling, about 200 logical qubits in 2029; Blue Jay, about 2,000 from 2033); Quantinuum (Apollo, fault-tolerant in 2029); Chakrabarti et al., Quantum 5, 463 (2021); Gidney, arXiv:2505.15917 (2025). Roadmaps are ambitions, not commitments. Details in the technology mapping, reviewed every month.

Spend the wait well

A quantum pricer will be judged against your best classical one. Everything that makes that benchmark strong pays off now, and it is the same work whether quantum arrives in 2032 or never.

  1. Benchmark what you run

    Independent reference values, documented errors, tests with criteria fixed in advance. The validated kits start here.

  2. Make it faster and safer

    Variance reduction, adjoint Greeks, richer models compared, findings closed. Gains you bank today.

  3. Decide with numbers

    A reduced case on a simulator, a resource estimate against your classical cost, and a dated go or no-go.
